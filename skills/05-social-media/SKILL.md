# Apparel Social Media Marketing

## Objective
Create credible content that demonstrates the current supplier's apparel development and manufacturing relevance to streetwear and independent fashion brands.

## Preparation
1. Read `config/company-profile.md` for the current company identity, website, products, target market, and confirmed capabilities.
2. If the profile is incomplete, avoid company-specific claims and ask for the missing details when necessary.
3. Use only genuine product images, factory footage, approved customer projects, and verifiable claims.

## Content Pillars
1. Product development: tech packs, sampling, fit, fabric selection, and details.
2. Craft and decoration: printing, embroidery, appliqué, washes, labels, and trims, only where capability is confirmed.
3. Quality and production: inspection, construction details, consistency, and packaging, using genuine evidence.
4. Buyer education: fabric weights, print methods, fit options, sampling preparation, and common production questions.
5. Brand collaboration: approved project stories and customer feedback only with permission.
6. Factory / team process: authentic behind-the-scenes content without exposing confidential customer information.

## Platform Adaptation
- **Instagram:** visual-first captions, concise hooks, relevant hashtags, clear profile/contact CTA.
- **Facebook:** practical explanations, product albums, community interaction, and direct inquiry prompts.
- **LinkedIn:** professional posts about product development, sourcing, quality processes, and B2B collaboration.

## Output for Each Post
- Platform
- Objective
- Target audience
- Visual / video concept
- Caption
- Call to action
- Hashtags (where suitable)
- Suggested posting format
- Claims requiring verification

## Rules
- Use platform-appropriate wording; do not paste the same caption everywhere without adaptation.
- Avoid hashtag stuffing and irrelevant trending tags.
- Do not use customer logos, proprietary graphics, or identifiable customer products without permission.
- Do not imply that a sample or production order belongs to a customer unless approved.
- Do not invent factory footage, customer results, certifications, production capacity, or quality statistics.
- Keep the CTA specific and low-pressure, such as inviting the viewer to share a tech pack or inquiry.
