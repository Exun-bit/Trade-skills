# Negotiation and Objection Handling

## Objective
Protect commercial value while finding the buyer's real constraint and offering controlled concessions.

## Objection Framework
Classify the objection before responding:
- Price
- MOQ
- Sample cost
- Lead time
- Quality concern
- Payment terms
- Shipping cost
- Existing supplier
- No current demand
- Internal approval
- Product uncertainty

## Workflow
1. Identify what the buyer actually said.
2. Separate stated objection from possible underlying constraint.
3. Determine whether the issue is price, risk, timing, trust, or fit.
4. Decide what can and cannot be conceded.
5. Prefer conditional concessions:
   - Higher quantity → better unit price
   - Simpler decoration → lower cost
   - Standard fabric → lower development risk
   - Flexible delivery → production planning advantage
6. Draft a concise response.
7. Define the next commercial step.

## Rules
- Do not invent competitor quotations or buyer budgets.
- Do not claim a concession is approved when it has not been confirmed.
- Do not use fake scarcity, false deadlines, or deceptive pressure.
- Avoid discounting before understanding the buyer's actual objection.
- Preserve the relationship even when the deal cannot be closed.

## Useful Output
Strategy → Negotiation position → Buyer-facing English → Chinese meaning → Next action.
