# Apparel Prospecting

## Objective
Build a focused, evidence-based prospect list for YuMu's custom clothing business.

## Workflow
1. Confirm the target market, product category, prospect type, and requested list size. If the user already supplied these, do not ask again.
2. Define an ideal customer profile (ICP): active apparel brand, relevant product range, plausible need for custom manufacturing, and a reachable business contact.
3. Discover prospects through official brand sites, social profiles, reputable directories, trade sources, and search engines.
4. Verify each brand is active. Prefer recent product launches, current social activity, live storefronts, or recent company updates.
5. Identify relevant products and customization cues: cut-and-sew garments, hoodies, tees, denim, outerwear, private-label collections, graphics, embroidery, washes, trims, and packaging.
6. Find public business contact channels. Prioritize sourcing, production, wholesale, founder, or general business contacts when their roles are verified.
7. Grade each lead A/B/C and explain the evidence behind the grade.
8. Recommend one practical next action and a follow-up date.

## Lead Scoring
- **A:** Strong product fit, active brand, credible evidence of custom apparel activity or recurring launches, and a usable business contact.
- **B:** Good product fit and active brand, but sourcing need or decision-maker contact is not confirmed.
- **C:** Weak fit, limited activity, unclear legitimacy, or no practical contact route.

## Output
Use a spreadsheet-friendly table with:
Brand | Website | Country | Product Fit | Evidence / Buying Signals | Contact Name | Role | Email | Instagram | LinkedIn | Lead Grade | Confidence / Unknowns | Suggested Opening Angle | Next Action | Follow-up Date

Do not fabricate missing fields. Use “Not found” or “Not verified”.

## Quality Checks
- Deduplicate brands and contacts.
- Confirm that the contact details belong to the brand or a clearly related business.
- Distinguish a public customer-service address from a verified sourcing contact.
- Do not interpret frequent social posts as proof of purchase intent.
- Cite sources for material claims when research tools provide citations.
