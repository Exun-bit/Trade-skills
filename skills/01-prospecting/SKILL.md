# Apparel Prospecting

## Objective
Build a focused, evidence-based prospect list for the user's current apparel business or represented supplier, including the best available email contact for each prospect.

## Workflow
1. Confirm the target market, product category, prospect type, and requested list size. If the user already supplied these, do not ask again.
2. Read `config/company-profile.md` when company-specific fit or positioning matters. If it is blank or outdated, ask for the missing current-company details rather than assuming.
3. Define an ideal customer profile (ICP): active apparel brand, relevant product range, plausible need for custom manufacturing, and a reachable business contact.
4. Discover prospects through official brand sites, social profiles, reputable directories, trade sources, and search engines.
5. Verify each brand is active. Prefer recent product launches, current social activity, live storefronts, or recent company updates.
6. Identify relevant products and customization cues: cut-and-sew garments, hoodies, tees, denim, outerwear, private-label collections, graphics, embroidery, washes, trims, and packaging.
7. Find and verify public business email contacts. Follow the Email Discovery Procedure below. Do not stop at providing only a website or social profile when an email search is feasible.
8. Identify relevant contacts such as founder, owner, CEO, sourcing, production, purchasing, or wholesale. Use a named person's email only when the person's identity, role, and email association are supported by evidence.
9. Grade each lead A/B/C and explain the evidence behind the grade.
10. Recommend one practical next action and a follow-up date.

## Email Discovery Procedure
Search in this order:
1. Official website: Contact, About, Wholesale, Stockists, Press, FAQ, and footer pages.
2. Official social profiles: business email shown in Instagram, Facebook, or LinkedIn profile/about sections.
3. Public company records, reputable business directories, and other credible public sources.
4. Hunter or another approved email-finding tool: search the brand's official domain, identify publicly indexed addresses and relevant named contacts, and inspect the tool's verification result.
5. If no address is found, report that no public email was found. Do not invent an address from a guessed naming pattern.

For every email, record:
- Exact email address.
- Source URL or tool/source name.
- Contact owner and role, if known.
- Address type: named decision-maker, role-based business inbox (for example info@, sales@, wholesale@), or general customer-service inbox.
- Verification status: Valid / Accept-all / Invalid / Unknown / Not verified.
- Verification method and date when available.

Verification rules:
- An email published on the brand's official website is a publicly listed address, but do not automatically call it deliverability-verified.
- A Hunter result is not automatically valid; preserve its actual verification status.
- “Accept-all” is not the same as confirmed deliverable.
- “Unknown” or “Not verified” must remain clearly labeled.
- Never describe a guessed email as verified.
- Prefer a relevant decision-maker's business email over a generic inbox when both are reliably available. If only a generic inbox is public, include it and label its type.
- Do not scrape, infer, or expose private personal email addresses. Use public professional contact information only.
- If tools or access are unavailable, state that the email could not be verified rather than implying the search was completed.

## Lead Scoring
- **A:** Strong product fit, active brand, credible evidence of custom apparel activity or recurring launches, and a usable business contact.
- **B:** Good product fit and active brand, but sourcing need or decision-maker contact is not confirmed.
- **C:** Weak fit, limited activity, unclear legitimacy, or no practical contact route.

## Output
Use a spreadsheet-friendly table with:
Brand | Website | Country | Product Fit | Evidence / Buying Signals | Contact Name | Role | Email | Email Type | Email Status | Email Source | Instagram | LinkedIn | Lead Grade | Confidence / Unknowns | Suggested Opening Angle | Next Action | Follow-up Date

Do not fabricate missing fields. Use “Not found” or “Not verified”.

## Quality Checks
- Deduplicate brands and contacts.
- Confirm that the contact details belong to the brand or a clearly related business.
- Distinguish a public customer-service address from a verified sourcing contact.
- Do not interpret frequent social posts as proof of purchase intent.
- Cite sources for material claims and email addresses when research tools provide citations.
