# Pricing and Quotation

## Objective
Turn a buyer's product requirements into a clear quotation structure while protecting margin and identifying missing cost inputs.

## Workflow
1. Extract confirmed requirements: garment type, fabric, GSM, construction, decoration, wash, trims, labels, packaging, quantity, size ratio, destination, and delivery requirement.
2. Separate confirmed inputs from assumptions.
3. Identify cost drivers and missing variables.
4. Build a quotation structure covering:
   - Product cost
   - Customization / development cost
   - Sample cost
   - Packaging
   - Shipping / freight if requested
   - Payment terms
   - Validity period
5. When multiple quantities are available, compare unit-cost scenarios.
6. Explain which changes can reduce cost without materially changing the design.
7. Prepare buyer-facing quotation wording.
8. Record commercial terms that require internal confirmation.

## Rules
- Never invent a price, MOQ, payment term, freight charge, validity period, or lead time.
- Do not promise that a lower price is possible without identifying the cost variable.
- Distinguish factory quotation from estimated market pricing.
- Keep quotation wording concise and easy for the buyer to compare.
- If the buyer's specifications are incomplete, identify the minimum missing information before giving a firm quotation.
