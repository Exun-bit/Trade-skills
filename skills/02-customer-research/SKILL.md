# Apparel Customer Research

## Objective
Prepare a concise brand brief that makes outreach specific to the prospect's products and positioning, and gives the user a reliable route to contact the business.

## Research Workflow
1. Review the official website, product pages, About page, shipping/returns information, and wholesale or contact pages.
2. Review recent Instagram, Facebook, and LinkedIn activity where available.
3. Identify core categories, signature silhouettes, fabrics, washes, prints, embroidery, appliqué, labels, trims, and packaging details visible in public materials.
4. Estimate positioning from observable product prices and presentation. Clearly label this as an estimate, not internal business data.
5. Look for launch frequency, seasonal drops, collaborations, restocks, and expansion into new categories.
6. Identify plausible manufacturing pain points only as hypotheses, such as consistency across drops, custom wash development, low-volume launches, or complex decoration.
7. Read `config/company-profile.md` and match the current supplier's value proposition only to capabilities confirmed in that profile or by reliable company information.
8. Research public business email addresses using official Contact/About/Wholesale/Press pages, official social profiles, credible public directories, and Hunter or another approved email-finding tool when available.
9. For each address, record its source, associated person/role if confirmed, whether it is a named or generic business inbox, and its actual verification status.
10. Prepare a personalized outreach angle and one concise opening message.

## Email Verification
- Prefer publicly listed business emails from the brand's own domain or official contact pages.
- Search for founder/owner/CEO/sourcing/production/wholesale contacts when relevant, but never assign a role without evidence.
- Use Hunter to discover domain emails and check its returned status when the tool is available.
- Separate “published on official website” from “deliverability verified”; these are different claims.
- Preserve statuses such as Valid, Accept-all, Invalid, Unknown, or Not verified exactly. Do not upgrade an uncertain result to Valid.
- Never guess an email from a naming convention and present it as real.
- If no email can be found, explicitly say “No public email found” and provide the best official alternative contact channel.
- Include source URLs so the user can inspect the contact information.

## Brand Brief Format
- Brand overview
- Main product categories
- Visual identity and customer positioning
- Observable construction / decoration details
- Recent launches or relevant activity
- Potential sourcing relevance
- Verified facts
- Research hypotheses (clearly labeled)
- Missing information
- Contact email(s), type, verification status, and source
- Relevant capabilities of the current supplier (verified only)
- Personalized first-contact message

## Rules
- Do not infer country of manufacture from a brand's country or shipping origin.
- Do not claim a brand uses a particular factory unless a reliable source confirms it.
- Do not treat retail price as proof of manufacturing budget.
- Do not invent order volume, supplier dissatisfaction, purchasing plans, or decision-maker identity.
- Keep the brief actionable; avoid collecting irrelevant personal information.
